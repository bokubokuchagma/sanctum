> "The sea remembers every spire drowned beneath its salt; it merely waits for men to forget how to swim."  
> *— First Chronicle of the Drowned Coast*

<p class="lead">The bell in the sunken tower rang three times before dawn, a low iron resonance that vibrated through the flagstones and up into the soles of Saren's boots. No one had pulled that hemp rope in forty years, not since the tide receded beyond the outer reef and left the harbor choked with white salt.</p>

He wiped the damp grease from his lens. Through the salt mist, the lighthouse silhouette leaned eastward at an unnatural pitch, like a needle searching for a pulse in the fog. Beside him, Vael did not blink; her fingers traced the copper grooves of the astrolabe, feeling for the magnetic shudder that always preceded a rift.

![The beacon lantern burned without oil, casting jagged geometry across the black shoal.](../assets/images/placeholder.svg)

"Listen," she whispered. The tone of the bell had shifted, losing its metallic bite and turning wet, like fingers dragging through still water. "It isn't signaling ships. It's calling something back."

---

They walked along the causeway where the black basalt blocks were slick with nocturnal lichen. In the distance, between two crags of fractured stone, a faint emerald seam pulsed in rhythm with the bell. Saren paused, his hand instinctively falling to the latch of his lantern.

"If the gate is opening," he said, keeping his voice beneath the wind, "the city council's scouts won't arrive before midday. We are alone out here, Vael."

She turned, a ghost of a smile touching her chiseled jaw under the hood of her waxed coat. "We have always been alone, archivist. That is why they sent us."
